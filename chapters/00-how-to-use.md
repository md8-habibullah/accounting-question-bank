# How to use this book

## The exam in one minute

The ICAB Certificate Level **Accounting** exam (as stated in the ICAB Accounting Workbook 2026):

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 1.5 hours |
| Questions | 50 questions, 2 marks each (100 marks) |
| Question types | Multiple choice (one answer) and multiple response (more than one answer) |
| Time per question | about 1 minute 48 seconds on average (90 minutes ÷ 50) |

The marks are shared out like this (the workbook says the real exam follows these weightings closely). The last column shows which chapters of this book feed each area (our mapping, for planning only).

| Syllabus area | Weight | Chapters |
|---|---:|---|
| Accounting concepts, ethics and sustainability | 15% | 1 |
| Maintaining financial records | 20% | 2, 3, 4 |
| Adjustments to accounting records and financial statements | 20% | 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 |
| Preparing financial statements | 40% | 5, 11, 12, 13, 14 |
| Public Financial Management | 5% | 15 |

> **KEY FACT** Preparing financial statements is **40%** of the marks. If you are short of time, protect chapters 5, 11, 12 and 13 first, and make sure the adjustments in chapters 6 to 10 are solid, because the statements depend on them.

## How every chapter is organised

1. **Learn** (about 20 to 30 minutes). Plain-English notes, key facts, formulas, worked examples, exam traps.
2. **Practise.** Do the questions in this order and keep the time limit of about 2 minutes per exam-style question:
    - the **ICAB workbook interactive questions** (full edition only);
    - the **ICAEW Question Bank questions** for the chapter (full edition only);
    - the **extra practice questions** and **true/false** in this book (Q1 to Q8 exam-style, Q9 and Q10 longer scenario questions with blank space for your working).
3. **Check.** Mark yourself on the answer pages. Read the **reason**, not just the letter. Write every mistake in a small notebook (your *mistake log*) and re-do those questions the next day.

> **REMEMBER** A wrong answer you understand is worth more than a right answer you guessed.

## How it all fits together (the big picture)

Every chapter is one step in this chain. When you feel lost, find the step.

| Step | What happens | Chapters |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Something happens | A sale, a purchase, a payment, wages are earned | 1, 2 |
| 2. A document is created | Invoice, credit note, receipt, payslip | 3 |
| 3. It is listed in a book of first entry | Day books, cash book, journal | 3 |
| 4. It is posted to the ledger (double entry) | Every debit has an equal credit | 4 |
| 5. A trial balance is drawn up | Checks that debits equal credits | 4, 5 |
| 6. Errors are found and corrected | Suspense account, correcting journals | 6 |

| 9. Public sector view | How governments record and report | 15 |

## Exam technique for multiple choice

- **Read the last line first.** Find what you are asked to calculate or choose, then read the facts.
- **For multiple response, count the answers needed.** "Select TWO" means exactly two. Tick all correct ones before moving on.
- **Calculation questions:** the wrong options are usually the answers of *common mistakes* (wrong sign, forgot the opening balance, used the wrong period). If your answer is in the list, check that you did not simply land on a trap. Do the working on paper; do not do it in your head.
- **Eliminate.** Cross out options you know are wrong. Often two are clearly wrong.
- **Time.** If a question takes more than about 3 minutes, mark it and come back. Answer every question before the end. Check the instruction page on exam day about how wrong answers are scored.
- **Statements:** for assets, liabilities and equity, always check the balance-sheet equation: assets = liabilities + equity.

## Notation used in this book

- Amounts are in **£**, as in the ICAEW exam-standard question bank. The ICAB workbook writes "CU" (currency units). The arithmetic is identical in any currency.
- **20X1, 20X2** simply mean "some year" (exam papers do this to avoid real dates).
- **Dr** = debit, **Cr** = credit. **b/d** = brought down (the opening balance), **c/d** = carried down (the closing balance).
- Brackets mean a negative or a deduction: (500).
- *Statement of profit or loss* is the income statement. *Statement of financial position* is the balance sheet.
